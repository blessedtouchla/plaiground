When the stage owns the pipe: what TikTok's Daddy Yankee deal means for independent creators

Nov 19, 2026. By PLAIGROUND.

On November 10, TikTok announced that SoundOn, its own distribution and promotion service, is handling the global distribution, artist services and promotion for Daddy Yankee's album La Postal. TikTok calls it SoundOn's "biggest artist partnership to date." [1]

That is a big name for a service TikTok launched in 2022 to, in its own words, "empower new and undiscovered artists." [1] For anyone releasing music, including people who write with super intelligence, the deal raises one question that matters more than the celebrity headline: what changes when the app where fans discover a song is owned by the same company that delivers it?

What actually happened?

Here is what the reporting confirms, and nothing more:

  • La Postal came out October 30 through DY Records, Daddy Yankee's label. SoundOn supports its global distribution, artist services and promotion. [1]
  • TikTok is running a campaign around it: a Times Square billboard, a profile frame fans unlock by completing tasks in the app, an unlockable video, a search "Easter egg" and an album hub. [1][2]
  • Financial terms were not disclosed. [2]
  • TikTok's announcement did not say who owns the master recordings. The album's streaming credits list DY Records as the owner of the sound recording copyright. [2]

SoundOn has worked with established acts before, including Snoop Dogg's Death Row catalog in 2023 and Alan Walker's Walkerworld 2.0 in January 2025. [1]

Why does "platform-owned distributor" matter?

Most releases have two separate jobs done by separate companies. A distributor delivers your song to stores and streaming services and pays you what comes back. A platform, like TikTok, is where people hear it and share it.

With La Postal, those jobs sit closer together. As Music Business Worldwide put it, TikTok "operates the app where fans discover the music and runs the in-app campaign that engages them," which gives one company a role in both distribution and promotion. [2] MBW has also noted that SoundOn leaves TikTok balancing the artists it distributes against the major-label music it also promotes. [2]

That is not an accusation. It is a structure, and structures shape incentives.

A very old question in a new app

Medieval lawyers had a phrase for this: nemo iudex in causa sua, no one should be a judge in their own cause. The idea is older than any record label. When the same hand that profits from an outcome also decides the outcome, even an honest hand deserves a second look.

A platform that both distributes music and decides what surfaces in its feed is, in a small way, judging its own cause. Maybe it does so fairly. The point of the old principle is that fairness should be visible, not assumed. For an artist, "visible" means written terms you can read before you sign.

There is a second, gentler lesson. Daddy Yankee's own description of the album is that each song is a different "love letter." [1] A love letter is only as good as the hands it passes through on the way. Picking a distributor is choosing the mail carrier for your work. You are allowed to ask questions about the route.

What should I ask any distributor, TikTok-owned or not?

Use these whether you are looking at SoundOn, PLAIGROUND, or anyone else:

Five questions to get in writing

  1. Who owns my master recording after I upload? Get it in writing. In the La Postal case, the credits name the artist's own label. [2]
  2. What share of my royalties do you keep, and does it change over time? At SoundOn's 2022 launch, MBW reported its FAQs said artists kept 100% of royalties on ByteDance platforms, and 100% on other services for the first year and 90% after that. [2] Those were launch terms. Check SoundOn's current terms yourself before relying on them.
  3. Is promotion tied to distribution? If a service promotes the music it distributes, ask how that affects music it does not distribute.
  4. How do you handle AI-made or AI-assisted songs? Ask what they accept, what they label, and what they will not deliver. See Human, AI-assisted or Fully AI and how platforms treat AI music.
  5. Can I leave, and what happens to my releases? Ask how a catalog is taken down, and whether you keep the files, the codes, and the credits.

Does this mean TikTok owns Daddy Yankee's album?

The reporting does not say that. TikTok says SoundOn supports La Postal's distribution, artist services and promotion. The album was released through DY Records, which the streaming credits list as the sound recording copyright owner. Deal terms were not disclosed. [1][2]

Is distribution the same as publishing?

No. Distribution gets your recording to stores. Publishing collects for the song itself. See Publishing is not distribution.
