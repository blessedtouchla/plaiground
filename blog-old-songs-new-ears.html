Old Songs, New Ears. Who Are You Actually Competing With?

Nov 9, 2026. The newest upload is not the only thing in the queue. Older recordings still take most of the listens.

A new song does not only compete with this week's releases. It competes with recordings people already know, and with a catalog that keeps getting played.

What Luminate counted

Luminate's 2025 year-end report says U.S. on-demand audio streams of current music, which it defines as tracks 18 months old or newer, were 334.0 billion in 2025, down from 339.3 billion in 2024. The same report says 43% of U.S. on-demand audio streams were from tracks released in the last five years, 2021 through 2025, and that this share was down from 49.6% for the previous five-year window in 2024. The Associated Press, citing that report, put the same 43% in plainer words: less than half of U.S. on-demand audio streams were tracks from those five years.

Luminate also says its release-year shares are based on the top 500,000 U.S. on-demand audio tracks, not on every file ever uploaded. Read the 43% as a picture of what got played, not as a census of what got delivered.

A careful distinction

Catalog share is not a moral verdict on new music, and it is not a royalty rate. A song from 1998 and a song from last month can sit in the same listener's queue. The distinction is ownership. The recording that gets a second life pays whoever still owns it, on whatever terms that owner actually has. A viral week does not transfer the master. A split that was never signed does not become clean because an old hook came back.

This post does not claim that more music is uploaded now than ever, and it does not repeat an anecdote about a particular family or a particular prom song. Those lines were not tied to a source we could check.

What to do on day one

  • Know who owns the master before the song has a second life.
  • Write the splits before the file leaves.
  • Make the recording something a stranger can understand without this month's trend. That is a craft bet, not a guarantee it will be rediscovered.

The philosophy underneath

You are not only in a race with your peers. You are in a library. The artists who last are the ones who still own the page when a new pair of ears finds it.

Sources: Luminate, 2025 Year-End Music Report; Associated Press, Jan 2026, citing Luminate.

Own the song before it has a second life.

Write the split. Keep the master if you can. No one can promise a rediscovery.
