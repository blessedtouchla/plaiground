The field

Brands do not buy a mystery file.

A sync license is the right to put a song under a picture. Ads. Film. Games. A creator video that is actually an ad. Two rights move: the composition and the recording. If either is fuzzy, the deal dies.

What they ask

Who wrote it. Who owns the master. What a human did. What AI did. Who signs.

What we send

The attestation. The signed split sheet. The release date. The plan the track shipped on.

What we do not sell

A public sync storefront. A buy-now card on the marketing site. You talk to PLAI. The packet lives in the account.

The packet

  • Tell us what a human did. No AI. AI-assisted. Fully AI. That box is not optional.
  • Everyone on the song signs the split sheet before delivery. We generate it. Co-writers get the email.
  • Traditional and AI-assisted tracks can sit in a normal clearance conversation. The paper is clean.
  • Fully AI still ships to stores. Some stores will not pay. Tidal may list and not pay. We do not invent a pay row.
  • Fully AI cannot opt in to Video Collect. Content ID is not a loophole.
  • No cloning existing artists. No dead-singer cover dressed up as a sync. That file never leaves the door.

Platform libraries for organic posts are not a commercial license. The second a brand boosts the clip, they need the real rights. That is why the packet exists before anyone asks.

Creator and Pro collect the recording and the song. Basic gets the first track out. Publishing and payouts wait on a paid plan.

PLAIGROUND takes none of the royalties. Membership is the fee. Stores still take their cut. We do not add another one.

If you can buy a car at the click of a button, you should be able to put a finished song in front of a supervisor the same way. The extra click is telling the truth.
