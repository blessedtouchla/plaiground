Wanna plai?

Labels have the AI money. The musicians who played still do not.

Nov 16, 2026. Human Made, Human Paid. No public deal after the November talks.

Labels are collecting AI licensing money.

The musicians who played on the records are not.

That is the core of the American Federation of Musicians’ Human Made, Human Paid campaign.

The pain points

Major labels have licensed catalogs to AI companies.

They receive the payouts.

Session players and recording musicians on those tracks receive nothing.

No consent.

No credit.

No share of the revenue.

The same labels that sue AI platforms to protect their master copyrights argue that the people who actually performed on the recordings have no claim under the existing contract.

The Sound Recording Labor Agreement already requires new-use payments when a recording is licensed for a new purpose.

The AFM says AI training and generation qualify.

The labels say they do not.

Four earlier rounds of bargaining stalled on this point.

What the AFM is proposing

Three non-negotiable items, written into the next SRLA:

Consent: musicians can approve or refuse use of their performances for AI training.

Credit: transparent recognition when their work is used.

Compensation: a direct share when catalog recordings generate value in AI licensing deals.

The campaign is explicit: this is not a fight against the technology or against tools for making music.

It is a demand that working musicians not be cut out of the money their recordings are now generating.

Where it stands and what happens next

The campaign launched in mid-October 2026, timed to the fifth round of SRLA talks with Universal, Sony, Warner, and Hollywood Records.

Those talks ran November 3–5.

As of mid-November there is no public announcement of an agreement.

The prior contract was extended through July 31, 2026. A new deal is still open.

In parallel, the AFM’s federal lawsuit against Universal, Warner Records, and Atlantic continues.

It claims the labels licensed member recordings to Suno and Udio without the required new-use notice or payment.

Anyone can sign the petition at humanmadehumanpaid.com.

The next public signal will be whether the November sessions produced contract language on the three Cs, or whether the fight moves to the next bargaining round and the courtroom.

Sources: AFM Human Made Human Paid site, Rolling Stone, Complete Music Update, AFM town hall notes.
