Suno Visuals: what your song can look like now

Nov 19, 2026. By PLAIGROUND.

Have you ever wanted to know what your song would look like, not only how it sounds? On November 17, 2026, Suno launched Visuals. You drag a song from your library into a new Visuals tab, and the tool can make lyric videos, album art, or lip-synced clips up to 15 seconds long. The clip can follow the song's structure, pick up the lyrics, and move a mouth with the vocal. After that, you can extend it, edit it, or re-prompt it with text.

For a beginner, that opens a door that used to look locked. You do not have to hire a video team to see your hook on a screen. You do have to decide whether the picture is yours, or just the first picture the machine offered.

In Book X of Plato's Republic, a painting is a copy of a copy: first the idea, then the made thing, then the image of the made thing. Here, a prompt becomes a song, and the song becomes a video. Each step can still carry your taste. Each step can also drift if you stop choosing.

If a machine can turn your song into a music video in 15 seconds, is the vision still yours, or are you just the audience of your own art?

What is Visuals, and what can it actually do?

Visuals is Suno's tool for images and short videos built around a song you already made. Suno's Chief Product Officer, Jack Brody, wrote that video clips "follow your song's structure, pick up on your lyrics and lip sync right along with them."

In plain words, that means the picture is supposed to listen to the track. A lyric video can move with the beat. A character can mouth the words. Album art can be generated to match the mood you describe. You can also start from an original image of your own, or from original characters Suno provides, and turn a still into a clip.

A first clip can be 5 to 15 seconds long. You pick the length and the shape, including vertical (9:16) for apps such as TikTok, plus square, landscape, and other ratios. Fast mode is 480p, meant for quick drafts. Standard is 720p, and it costs more Visual credits.

Visual credits are separate from Song credits. Anybody can buy Visual credit packs, and purchased packs do not expire. For a limited time, Pro and Premier subscribers get a limited grant of Visual credits to try the tool. Suno has not published that grant amount on the public help pages we checked, so this post does not invent a number.

Suno says Visuals launched on web, iOS, and Android. If you do not see it in the app, Suno's help pages say to update to the latest version.

This post does not claim a new commercial license for the clips themselves. Commercial use of a Suno song still follows Suno's usual plan and download rules. Read those terms before you sell or promote anything. For the wider November feature set, see Suno's November 2026 update.

What does that open up for someone just getting started?

It opens a way to test a hook with your eyes. You can cut a 15-second clip to the line you care about, see if the lyric lands, and try a different picture without starting over from scratch. You can make a cover image before you have a designer. You can extend a short clip into a slightly longer one, and keep prompting until the scene is closer to what you meant.

It does not open a shortcut around taste, rights, or honesty. The tool will generate something. Your job is to decide what is worth keeping, and to say how it was made.

Have you ever wanted to know who directed the clip?

Roland Barthes wrote about the death of the author: once a work is out in the world, the maker's private intent is not the last word. Michel Foucault asked a sharper question: what is an author, if a work can circulate without a single clear source? A Visuals clip makes that old question feel new. Who directed this video? You, if you chose the song, the hook, the character, the camera, and the cuts. The model, if you accepted the first draft and hit share.

Keep the direction. Write the hook first, and lock it. Song Lab is one place to do that work before you generate. In Visuals, describe the character, the place, the action, and the camera, rather than a vague mood. For lip-sync, pick a stretch of audio with clear singing or speech, and keep the face and mouth in the frame. Draft in Fast. If the idea holds, run Standard. After the first clip, use Edit, Extend, or a new prompt to push it toward the scene you had in mind. The machine can propose. You still have to choose.

What should you know about the lawsuits in the background?

Suno's own FAQ says the model behind Visuals "is called H3 Move, based on H3 from MiniMax," and that Suno optimized it for its community, "including adding additional protections and safeguards." Music Business Worldwide reports that MiniMax is facing a copyright lawsuit brought by Disney, Comcast's Universal (the film studio, not Universal Music Group), and Warner Bros. Discovery. That case is ongoing. It is not a ruling on Visuals, and it is not a ruling on your song.

Suno says it built Visuals with new text and visual filters "intended to block uploads and prompts that try to use or create visual representations of specific artists, public figures, copyrighted works and IP." Brody wrote that "prompts that work on other video platforms may not work on Suno and that's by design." Do not prompt a real artist's face, a public figure, or a copyrighted character. Use an original character, or an image you have the rights to. The first time you upload a file, Suno asks you to confirm those rights.

Immanuel Kant argued that people should be treated as ends, not as means. A filter that blocks a real person's likeness is a small version of that idea: you do not get to borrow a face as a prop. But a filter is not a settlement. It sits on top of a disputed foundation, a model family and a company still in court over training data. Use the safeguard. Do not confuse it with a final answer from a judge.

The same day Visuals was announced, Music Business Worldwide reported that Universal Music Group and Sony Music Entertainment had opposed Suno's bid to pause their second copyright lawsuit. That second case, filed on September 18, 2026, covers 60,202 recordings, according to the reporting. In papers filed on November 13, the labels argued that under Suno's plan, its 2025 and 2026 models and business conduct, including the v6 models, would be "examined nowhere until at least 2028." That is the labels' claim about a requested pause, not a finding that the models will escape review. The cases are ongoing. This is not legal advice. For how rights and labels work when you release, see copyright and AI labels.

Can a Visuals clip work as a Spotify Canvas?

Spotify Canvas is a short vertical loop that shows in the Now Playing view instead of album art. Spotify's own Canvas guidelines say a Canvas needs to be 3 to 8 seconds long, vertical in a 9:16 ratio, between 720 and 1080 pixels tall, and an MP4 or a JPG file. Spotify also asks you to choose footage without talking, singing, or rapping, because a clip that short will not stay in sync with the lyrics.

A Canvas-shaped Visuals clip

  • Pick 9:16 (vertical).
  • Keep lip-sync off, so no talking, singing, or rapping on screen.
  • Trim the clip to 3 to 8 seconds.
  • Export a quiet file between 720 and 1080 pixels tall, as an MP4 or a JPG.

If you follow those steps, the file can fit those Canvas guidelines. That is a file-shape check, not a promise that Spotify will accept every clip, and not a claim that a Canvas will raise streams. Before you use a Visuals clip off Suno, read Suno's terms. This post does not claim that Visuals gives you a commercial license for the clip.

How do you use Visuals well, and stay honest?

Start from the hook, not the scenery. Open a song's action menu, choose Create Visual, then Video Clip, or go to Create, then Visuals, then Video. Add the section of the song you want. Turn on Lip Sync if you want the mouth to follow the vocal. To put lyrics on the clip, open it and turn on Show Lyrics. Keep records as you go: the date, your plan, the prompt, the song section, the Visual you kept, and any image you uploaded. A simple note can help later if a platform or a rights question comes up.

A listener should not have to guess. If super intelligence (SI) helped make the record or the picture, say so.

TikTok's Music Terms (effective January 2026) say that if you upload an AI Music Video, you should provide clear disclosures. Captions or hashtags such as "AI generated music" are examples TikTok gives, and you can add TikTok's AI-generated content label. TikTok also says it can auto-label some uploads that carry C2PA Content Credentials. Suno says it applies an embedded watermark before a Visual can be shared off its platform. Whether that watermark is a Content Credential, or whether it will trigger TikTok's auto-label, is unknown. Do not wait to find out. Turn the label on yourself, and write a clear caption. You should not use a generated vocal to fake a real person's voice, or put a real person's likeness in the clip without the permission the law requires.

When you post the song on YouTube, see How to disclose AI music on YouTube. When you release, use PLAIGROUND's labels exactly as they are written: Human, AI-assisted, or Fully AI. A generated Suno track with a Visuals clip is not Human. This is not a claim that plai or PLAIGROUND passes those labels, or any SI or AI credits, to TikTok, YouTube, or other stores and DSPs. You mark the post yourself.

If you want a place to start, join wannaplai free for starter credits. Write the hook. Shape the picture. Keep the credit honest.

FAQ

Does Visuals give me a commercial license for the clip? We are not claiming that. Commercial use of a Suno song still follows Suno's usual plan and download rules. Read Suno's terms before you sell or promote a clip.

Do I have to tell people the video was made with super intelligence? On TikTok, if you upload an AI Music Video, the Music Terms say you should provide clear disclosures, and you can add the AI-generated content label. On YouTube, follow the Studio disclosure steps in our guide. On a release, use an honest Human, AI-assisted, or Fully AI label.

Can I make a clip that looks like a famous artist? No. Do not try. Suno says its new filters are meant to block prompts and uploads that use a specific artist's likeness, a public figure, or copyrighted work. Use an original character, or an image you have the rights to.
