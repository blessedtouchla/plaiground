Surviving the Silence: The Real Test of an Artist

Nov 4, 2026

Talent gets you started. Handling the quiet keeps you going.

Most careers don't end because the music is bad. They end because the artist can't handle long stretches of being ignored.

Silence isn't a verdict

No streams. No comments. No callbacks.

Your brain reads that as danger. It isn't. Usually it just means nobody has found the song yet.

Durability is a habit

The industry isn't only looking for talent. It wants people who keep making music after the numbers drop and the rejections stack up.

Virtue ethics says courage isn't a feeling you wait for. You build it by acting. Show up enough times and you become durable.

Split your world in two

The Stoics sorted life into two piles:

  • Not up to you:

    the algorithm, playlist editors, timing.

  • Up to you:

    the next song, the next session, the systems that carry you through bad days.

Put your worth in the second pile. Then silence is just where you do the work.

Silence is just where you do the work.

Hype can hurt too

A big viral moment can hit as hard as a long drought. When the attention fades, the drop can feel like grief.

If you need validation to keep going, you're only renting your success. If you build it on daily practice, you own it.

You're not late

The absurdists picture a man rolling a boulder uphill and watching it roll back down, forever. The point is that the pushing itself can be the meaning.

Nobody gets a schedule, so you can't be behind one. All you have is the next push. Keep your promises to yourself one release at a time.

Build for the long quiet
