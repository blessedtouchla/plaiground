Let the Bots Do the Drudgery

Oct 15, 2026. What AI should actually do for artists, so you can make the choices only you can make.

Every post in this series has circled one idea. AI is very good at repeating what already exists. It is not good at inventing what does not exist yet. Chris Miller called it “a copying machine” and hoped it would be used “as a tool to simplify some drudgery.”

So let’s take that seriously. If a copying machine is good at repeatable work, what repeatable work in a music career should it be doing?

Quite a lot, it turns out.

The work nobody became an artist to do

Ask any independent artist what eats their week, and you will hear a familiar list:

  • Filling out distribution forms and metadata, then fixing them when something gets rejected.
  • Chasing collaborators to agree on who owns what percentage of a song.
  • Reading contracts and wondering what a clause actually means.
  • Remembering to register copyright and publishing.
  • Checking that a release is truly ready before it goes out.
  • Keeping track of dates, deadlines, and lead times.
  • Working out where the royalties landed and why.

Every one of those matters. Skip a split sheet and you can end up in an argument years later. Rush a release and you can lose your best chance at a strong first week. But none of them require your unique point of view. They require accuracy, consistency, and patience. That is exactly what software is good at.

What stays with you

Here is the list of things we would never want a bot to decide for you:

  • What the song is about.
  • Which story you are brave enough to tell.
  • The odd genre pairing that makes your sound yours.
  • The lyric that is a little too specific, and perfect because of it.
  • How you want to show up in the world.

Those are the fox-cardigan choices from earlier in this series. The details that come from a life. Miller said AI could not have made the first Spider-Verse because “there was nothing like it to have taken from.” The same is true of your best song. It does not exist anywhere yet, which is exactly why it has to come from you.

Tool, not replacement

We serve human artists and AI-assisted artists alike, and we are not interested in drawing lines based on which software you have open. The line we care about is simpler. Is the tool doing the drudgery so the artist can do the art? Or is it trying to be the artist?

When the bots handle the forms, the filings, the splits, and the deadlines, you get your time back. And time is what original work is made of.
