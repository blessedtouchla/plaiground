If you couldn't tell who made the song, would it still matter?

Nov 22, 2026. By PLAIGROUND.

Draft for Victoria's review. Not published, not scheduled, and not legal advice. Facts were checked on November 22, 2026, at about 4:30 PM PT (America/Los_Angeles), against the posts and pages listed at the end. Leaderboard scores change. Read the sources.

Have you ever wanted to know if a song can win when nobody knows who made it?

On November 12, 2026, at 10:24 AM PT (America/Los_Angeles), ACE Music posted that ACE Music v1 had hit number one on Artificial Analysis' music leaderboard, and that it was "the first model on this chart to outrank Suno's latest release." They invited people to "try it free." That is their claim. A live scoreboard is the place to check it, because Elo numbers move.

What the scoreboard showed on November 22

On November 22, 2026, at about 4:30 PM PT (America/Los_Angeles), the AA-Music-Vocal v1.1 leaderboard listed ACE Music v1 first, at 1160 Elo, plus or minus 20, with a 95 percent interval from 1140 to 1180, from 2,128 samples. Suno v6 was second, at 1145, plus or minus 18, with a 95 percent interval from 1127 to 1163, from 3,073 samples. The printed rank ranges were 1 to 2 for ACE Music v1, and 1 to 3 for Suno v6.

Those intervals overlap. The lead was real on the board as of that check, and it was narrow: a fifteen-point gap with ranges that cross.

The test is a blind comparison. Evaluators hear two tracks generated from the same prompt and choose one, without knowing which model made which song. Elo scores come from those votes by Artificial Analysis' validated private evaluators. The vocal board is for music with singing or other vocal elements. A second board, AA-Music-Instrumental v1.1, is for music without vocals. On that board, as of the same check, Suno v6 was still first, at 1148, plus or minus 22, with a 95 percent interval from 1126 to 1170, from 2,208 samples, and a rank range of 1. ACE Music v1 was not listed there. A vocal lead is not a lead in music as a whole.

What ACE Music says it can do

ACE Music, the service, says you can generate complete songs from text, lyrics, MIDI, or a hummed melody. The ACE Music v1 product page is narrower: that model accepts text descriptions and lyrics. A separate mode, ACE Music v1.1 Preview, is described as generating songs from melodies and scores. If you try it, read which mode you are using.

ACE says you can try the product for free. Their November 12 post said, "Try it free." Their v1 page says songs generated on a free plan do not have commercial use rights, and that upgrading later does not grant those rights retroactively. Songs generated on a paid plan, they say, do. Check their terms before you release anything.

On open weights, keep two claims apart. In the November 12 post, ACE Music wrote, "We'll open-source the model weights in the next few weeks. We're making the final tweaks so you can run it locally." On the ACE Music v1 page, checked on November 22, they wrote that they "plan to open-source ACE Music v1 Flash in late 2026," and that Flash is a different balance of quality, speed, and hardware than ACE Music v1. We cannot confirm a date, a file, or that the original timeline still holds. Treat both lines as ACE's claims.

What a blind test can, and cannot, tell you

Immanuel Kant described a judgment of taste as disinterested: you are not asking who made the work, or whether it flatters your side, but how it stands as it is heard. A blind listening test is a rough, modern picture of that idea. Hide the logo, hide the name, and let the ear decide. Under those conditions, a machine was preferred often enough to sit at the top of a vocal board, at least on the day we checked. That is a fair kind of win, as far as sound preference goes.

Plato gave a colder story about art. In the Republic, a painting of a bed is a copy of a bed, which is already a copy of the idea of a bed: a copy of a copy. Music made with super intelligence can be read in that light, as a likeness of styles that were already likenesses of lived music. If that is all you see, a high Elo is only a more convincing shadow.

Both thoughts can be true at once. A blind test can let a model win fairly on what listeners prefer. It cannot tell you whether you still care who stands behind the work once the names are put back on the file. Preference is not the whole of meaning, and origin is not the whole of beauty.

For creators

  • Label the work honestly as Human, AI-assisted, or Fully AI.
  • Keep your prompts and stems as receipts.
  • Credit every collaborator.
  • Tell licensees and collaborators up front.
  • Check the metadata before you hand files off.
  • PLAIGROUND sends the AI label to stores and takes no commission to release your music.
