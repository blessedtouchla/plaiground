Nobody Will Run Your Career Better Than You. Are You Watching?

Nov 9, 2026. You can hire great people. You cannot hire someone to care more than you do.

A manager, a lawyer, a label, or a distributor can be good at the job and still not watch your career the way you will. That is not an insult. It is the shape of the work. They have many clients. You have one name.

What you are already doing

The moment a song earns money, there is a small business attached to it, whether or not you call it that. You do not need a business degree to see the moving parts. You do need to know they exist.

  • Recordings. Who owns the master, and for how long?
  • Songs. Who wrote what, and are the shares written down?
  • Where money can come from. Stores, performance royalties, sync, shows, merch, fans who pay you directly. None of these is a promise of a check.
  • Where money goes. Commissions, fees, an advance you have to earn back, taxes.
  • Contracts. How long they last, what they cover, and how you leave.
  • Metadata. Credits and registrations are how a royalty finds a person. If you are not registered, there is no account for that royalty to land in.
  • Audience. Fans you can reach without a platform's permission.

A careful distinction

Paying attention is not the same as doing every job yourself. A music attorney and an accountant are worth having. The distinction is that a team works better when the person whose name is on the song has read the paper. "Unregistered money often goes unclaimed" is a slogan, not a measured total, so this post does not use it. The plain version is enough: if you never sign up, the society has nowhere to send your share.

A checklist, not a guarantee

  1. Read a contract, then have a music attorney read it. Ask until you understand it.
  2. Put songwriter and producer shares in writing before the song comes out.
  3. Register the rights you actually have, with a performing rights organization and anywhere else the song needs an account.
  4. Look at statements. Where the money came from, where it went, and what does not add up.
  5. Know what each person on the team is paid, and how the arrangement ends.
  6. Keep your own copies of contracts, credits, and account ownership.
  7. Keep a fan list you control. Email or a community. Followers on an app are rented.

This is general information, not legal or financial advice.

The philosophy underneath

Talent gets you heard once. Literacy is what lets you tell, later, what you still own. You do not have to love the business. You cannot afford to be surprised by it.

No outside statistic in this post. The checklist is a practice. Related on this site: who made it, split sheets, and song royalties explained.
