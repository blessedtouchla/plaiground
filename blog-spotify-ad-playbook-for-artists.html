Spotify Wrote Brands a Playbook. What Should an Artist Keep?

Nov 9, 2026. The ad page is about attention. The artist version is a release system, not a media budget.

Spotify Advertising published an Ads Manager Performance Playbook for brands. The public page is not shy about the asset. It says listeners stream music and podcasts for an average of more than two hours a day, and that 93% of the engagement fans have with content on Spotify "extends to" engagement with ads.

What the page actually measures

On the playbook page, Spotify says campaigns that turn on its Pixel and Conversions API from day one see 22% lower cost per acquisition than the median across its campaigns. It says campaigns that use both audio and video see 63% higher conversion rates than the platform median. Those are advertiser results, compared with other Spotify ad campaigns. They are not artist royalty rates, and they are not a promise that a music video will raise your streams by 63%.

The same page describes the guide as covering measurement, format strategy, bidding, and creative, "at every stage of the campaign lifecycle." It does not, on that page, publish a five-step checklist with those names. Other percentages that circulate from the downloadable PDF, including cost-per-click and cost-per-thousand drops, are not repeated on the page we could read, so they are not in this post.

The artist translation

You can steal the discipline without buying the ad.

  • One goal per release. Saves, a signup, or a show. Not "something good."
  • Tracking before release day. A link you can read, and a list you own.
  • More than one format. A song and a cover are a file. A hook clip, a short video, and a consistent look are a campaign. The 63% figure is about ads. The lesson that is safe to keep is simpler: do not ship audio alone and call it a rollout.
  • A written lane. Genre, mood, and the artists your listeners already play. "Everyone" is not an audience.
  • A weekly look at Spotify for Artists, the links, and the list. Change one thing at a time. A quiet weekend is not a verdict.

A careful distinction

Paying Spotify, or any platform, for an ad that sends people to a signup you control is advertising. Paying a stranger for streams or a guaranteed playlist is the thing Spotify forbids. Its support page says services that promise streams or playlist placement for money violate its terms. Artificial streams do not earn royalties. The playbook's conversion math does not apply to a bought stream, and a bought stream can put the catalog at risk.

The philosophy underneath

Brands get a playbook because they pay for attention and then measure it. An artist already makes the thing the attention is for. The part worth keeping is the system: one goal, more than one format, a number you can read, and a list the platform cannot switch off.

Source: Spotify Advertising, Ads Manager Performance Playbook 2026, read Nov 9, 2026. The 22%, 63%, 93%, and two-hour figures are Spotify's own claims on that page. Playlist and stream-buying rules: Spotify support.
