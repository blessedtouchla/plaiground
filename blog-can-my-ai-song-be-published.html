Can my AI song be published? Full AI vs AI-assisted, explained.

Oct 23, 2026. What can get published, what can't, and how to tell where your song lands.

The short version is that a fully AI song can still be released, but it can't be registered as a songwriter work, because there's no human writer to register. Publishing pays songwriters, so it only covers the parts a person actually wrote.

Why fully AI songs can't be published

ASCAP, BMI and SOCAN put out a joint policy on Oct 28, 2025. They accept songs that are partly AI as long as a human wrote real parts of them. They don't accept songs made entirely by AI. BMI says it plainly: a song you only prompted doesn't count, because typing "make a sad R&B song about rain" isn't writing the lyrics or the melody. The US Copyright Office takes the same view. Copyright needs a human author, and a prompt alone isn't authorship.

What can get published

  • You wrote the lyrics yourself and used AI for the music or the vocals.
  • You wrote or hummed the melody and AI helped produce it.
  • AI gave you a draft and you rewrote it heavily, changing lines, the hook and the structure, so the final words are yours.
  • You co-wrote with a human and used AI as a tool along the way.

In every case, you register only the human part, and it's smart to be upfront that AI was used. BMI recommends disclosing it.

How to tell if your song qualifies

Ask yourself three questions:

  1. Did I write the lyrics, or change them so much that they're mine? If yes, you likely qualify.
  2. Did I create the melody or the hook myself? If yes, you likely qualify.
  3. If someone asked me to show my work, could I? That means drafts, notes, voice memos or lyric edits. Proof like that protects you if anyone ever challenges it.

If all three answers are no and you just typed a prompt and kept what came out, it doesn't qualify for publishing. You can still release it, but there are no songwriter royalties to collect.

One more thing

One more thing to know is that registering with BMI is not the same as filing a copyright. They're separate steps.
