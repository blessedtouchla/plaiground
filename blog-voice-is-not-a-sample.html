Your voice is not a sample.

Sep 25, 2026. Artists, labels and lawmakers started drawing a line between using a voice and taking one. General information, not legal advice.

Three things live in every vocal

The song. The recording. The voice.

Melody and lyrics are the composition. The audio file is the master. The sound that makes a singer recognizable as them is the voice. Songs and recordings are copyright. A voice, on its own, mostly is not. The U.S. Copyright Office said so in July 2024 and asked Congress for a new federal replica right. Until then, voices live mainly in state publicity law — the same family as Midler v. Ford. Owning a song, or even a master, does not automatically let you mint new vocals in the singer’s voice.

Artists who went first

Holly+ (2021): Herndon put a digital twin in a DAO so people could perform through her voice with official approval. Elf.Tech (2023): Grimes offered her voice commercially for half the master royalties, “be tasteful,” block extremes. Kits Earn: verified models, pay when users download.

Labels: sue, then license

UMG settled with Udio Oct 29, 2025. Warner settled with Udio Nov 19 and Suno Nov 25, 2025. Opt-in language for name, image, likeness, voice. As of late Sep 2026 (LAT): Suno has licensed Warner, BMG, Believe models. Udio has UMG, Warner, Merlin, Kobalt. Sony and Universal still suing Suno. Sony filed again against Udio. Pay and opt-in counts were not disclosed.

A label deal is not an artist yes

AFM sued Universal and Warner, alleging member recordings were licensed to AI without paying those musicians. Labels moved to dismiss. One advisor in the LAT piece: a label making a deal and an individual artist having a choice are not the same thing.

The laws. Voice becomes a named right.

  • Tennessee ELVIS Act, signed March 21, 2024, effective July 1, 2024. Adds voice to the state personal-rights statute.
  • California AB 2602: contracts that give away an AI digital replica of a performer’s voice or likeness have to spell it out. The performer needs professional representation in that negotiation.
  • California AB 1836: no commercial use of a deceased performer’s digital replica, including in sound recordings, without the estate.
  • Federal NO FAKES Act of 2026 (S. 4591) introduced May 20, 2026. Senate Judiciary advanced it in June. On the Senate calendar as of June 24, 2026. Not passed. Not law. Details can change.

What this means

  • Singers: read any line that says digital replica, AI, voice model, likeness. Opt-in should mean opt-in.
  • Creators: using a real singer without permission is getting riskier. Work with people who said yes.
  • If you own masters: that is not automatically a license to generate new vocals in the singer’s voice. Ask.
