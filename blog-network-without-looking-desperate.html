'Yo, Check My Music' Is Not Networking. So What Is?

Nov 9, 2026. A cold link is spam. A relationship is a person who already knows the work.

Most artists have received the message. "Yo, check my music." Then a link. No hello. No sign the sender heard anything. Most people do not open it. That is not a network. It is a blast.

What happened

The useful part of a network is not a follower count. It is a few people in the same lane who answer, because you showed up for their work before you asked for yours. Artists, producers, playlist curators who program for taste rather than a fee, and fans who already care: those are the rooms. A suggested way to start, not a research finding, is to look for people you can actually reach. Active. In your lane. Not so much larger that a message disappears. Any specific follower range is a guess. Throw it out if it does not match your scene.

A careful distinction

A relationship is not a transaction, and a playlist fee is not a relationship. Spotify's own support page says: "Any service that claims to offer guaranteed placement on playlists on Spotify in exchange for money are in violation of our terms & conditions, and they shouldn't be used." Spotify for Artists adds: "Spotify strictly prohibits using any third party service that promises streams or playlist placement in exchange for money." It also says to be highly skeptical of services that charge only to be "considered" for a playlist.

When Spotify confirms artificial streaming, it says those streams do not earn royalties, do not count toward public numbers or charts, and do not help recommendations. Depending on the case, the song can come off playlists. Spotify shares reports with labels and distributors, who may warn you or, in repeated cases, remove the music.

The free route is different. In Spotify for Artists you can pitch one upcoming, unreleased song to editors. Pitching does not guarantee a playlist. If you pitch at least seven days before release, Spotify says the song also goes to your followers' Release Radar. A separate Spotify for Artists release guide asks for at least two weeks, so editors have more time. Use the longer window when you can. It still costs nothing, and it still is not a promise.

A 90-day practice, not a promise

This is a calendar you can try. It is not a study, and it is not a quota.

  • Weeks 1 and 2: write down 20 people in your lane whose work you actually like.
  • Weeks 3 and 4: say something specific about that work before you mention yours.
  • Weeks 5 through 8: follow up like a person, not a schedule blast.
  • Weeks 9 through 12: keep the few conversations that came back.

A hope of a few real collaborators by the end is a hope. Some seasons land none. The point is unanswered-link piles are not a crew.

The philosophy underneath

Algorithms filter. A person who will say your name in a room does not. Visibility on a platform is rented. A relationship you kept is not.

Sources: Spotify, paid third-party services; Spotify for Artists, artificial streaming; Spotify, pitching to editors; Spotify for Artists release guide. The 90-day calendar is a practice, not a finding.
