The Music Business Is Not Cooked. Where Did the Money Move?

Nov 9, 2026. A bank forecast says the pie gets larger. A distributor's own survey says most of its artists still earn very little. Both can be true.

Comment sections like to say the music business is finished. Streams pay little. The majors win. The forecasts the banks publish do not describe a dying market. They describe a market that is trying to charge its heaviest listeners more, and to sell to them directly.

What the forecasts actually say

Music Ally, reporting Goldman Sachs' 2026 Music in the Air, says the bank projects global music revenue, across recorded music, publishing, and live, rising from $112.5 billion in 2025 to $198.1 billion in 2035. That is a forecast, not money already collected. Goldman's own 2026 research note says the long-term outlook was raised, and that the analysts are factoring in faster subscriber growth and more regular price increases. Price increases are one input. They are not the whole story, and they mostly help whoever already collects the subscription.

A separate, earlier estimate should not be mixed into that sentence. Music Business Worldwide, on June 3, 2025, reported that the 2025 edition of the same report estimated a potential annual uplift of $4.3 billion from superfan monetization, based on 2026 projections. The estimate assumed that 20% of paid subscribers could be treated as superfans and that they would spend twice what an average subscriber spends. It is a model, not a receipt.

What Universal told investors

At its September 17, 2024 Capital Markets Day, Universal Music Group said superfans, the most avid 20% to 30% of listeners, once drove more than 70% of recorded-music spending. The word that matters is once. UMG's point, as MBW reported it, was that streaming flattened that gap, because a casual listener and a heavy listener pay the same subscription. UMG's own materials put direct-to-consumer revenue growth at about a 33% compound annual rate from 2021 to 2023. That is a past rate. It is not a claim that the business is still growing at 33%.

On February 18, 2026, UMG announced a multi-year agreement with EVEN, a direct-to-fan platform, so labels and artists can sell early access, extras, and merchandise. MBW reported that UMG had already named superfan monetization a 2026 priority. Organizations can build that pipe. An independent artist does not need their permission to keep a list.

Fans are already in rooms. That is not the same as paying you.

Pew Research Center, on October 14, 2026, said nearly four in ten U.S. adults are part of an online community. Roughly one in ten are in a community about movies, books, or TV, and Pew said a similar share are in fan communities of any kind. Among people in an online community, 56% said they go to the one they visit most every day. About six in ten said that most-visited community is something they could only find online. Pew did not say those people are looking for a paid fan club, and it did not study music specifically.

One direct sale, reported carefully

Billboard, on January 5, 2026, reported that an independent rapper selling an album directly brought in $57,000 from about 2,600 fans in the first 24 hours, that one buyer paid $11,001, and that the average sale then was about $22, against a floor of $1. This post does not name the artist or the buyers. One weekend is not a career, and a famous buyer's check is not a plan. It is evidence that a direct price can exist beside streaming. It is not evidence that your next release will do the same.

The other number

Ditto Music said on September 29, 2026 that 74% of the artists in its Independent Music Report earned under $1,000 from music in 2025. The sample was 5,039 people in 112 countries, and every one of them had released music through Ditto. That is not every independent artist. It is a vendor's survey of its own releasers. Read it as a warning about that group, not as the world's average.

The philosophy underneath

A bigger industry forecast does not mail you a check. A direct line is a list you can write to when a recommendation changes. Treat the top of your own audience as people with names. Give them a place you control. Offer something a stream does not include. Sell directly once, learn from the buyers you actually have, and do not invent a royalty the forecast never promised.

Sources linked above. Goldman figures are forecasts and models. The UMG 33% figure is a 2021-2023 compound rate. The 70% spending line is historical, as UMG told it. Pew is about online communities, not music income. The Billboard sale is one reported weekend. Ditto's 74% is its own customer survey.

A forecast is not a paycheck.

Keep a list you control. Label the music honestly. No one can promise what a song will earn.
